Ventures & Market Entry

Higher Education

India–Bulgaria Education Corridor

A strategy report for a European university's India market entry: demand analysis, unit economics, counselor network architecture, execution roadmap, and risk matrix.

Delivered
Read the full case study

The brief

A European university wanted to know whether India was worth building a dedicated recruitment channel for — and if so, how to structure it rather than relying on ad-hoc agent referrals.

The approach

Sized the addressable demand by city tier and discipline, modeled unit economics per enrolled student against counselor commission and marketing spend, and mapped what a counselor network would need to look like to run profitably at scale.

The output

A full strategy report covering market sizing, counselor network architecture, an execution roadmap with phased milestones, and a risk matrix — delivered alongside a condensed pitch deck for leadership and a one-page reference card for counselors on the ground.

Fashion & Clothing

Poshaaki — Banka Silk, Positioned for Premium

Brand strategy and market entry for a GI-tagged Banka silk label targeting metro Indian women and the diaspora — a customer-first narrative that leads with the wearer, not the supply chain.

In build
Read the full case study

The brief

Banka silk is GI-tagged and genuinely premium, but most handloom labels selling it lead with the artisan's story and the supply chain — and lose the urban buyer who's actually deciding what to wear, not where the thread came from.

The approach

Mapped the competitive set on price, narrative, and channel, then rebuilt the positioning around the wearer first: occasion, fit, and how the fabric feels and photographs, with provenance as a supporting detail rather than the headline.

Where it stands

Brand strategy and positioning are set; currently building out sourcing relationships and the first collection ahead of launch.

Why the rigor is different

Depth

Before strategy work, there was lab work. As a Gold Medalist M.Tech researcher at MNIT Jaipur, I was first in the department to synthesize and characterize Graphene Quantum Dots — a multi-step hydrothermal process where a single mistake at any stage means starting over. The work also covered synthesizing reduced graphene oxide through a three-step hydrothermal route, graphene oxide via Hummer's method, and ZnO nanoparticles via sol-gel synthesis, each fully characterized using UV-Vis spectroscopy, AFM, FESEM, HRTEM, and Raman spectroscopy.

That kind of work trains a specific habit: build the process before you build the result, and don't claim a finding you can't reproduce. It's the same habit that shows up now in programme governance — structured reporting, defined ownership, a roadmap someone can actually follow — rather than strategy decks that look good and don't survive contact with delivery.

An MBA from IIM Jammu (with an exchange semester at the University of Bradford, UK) added the commercial and stakeholder layer on top of that research discipline. The entrepreneurial work — across cross-border exports, D2C brand building, and cultural tourism strategy — added the layer that formal education rarely teaches: what it costs to be wrong when it's your own money.
